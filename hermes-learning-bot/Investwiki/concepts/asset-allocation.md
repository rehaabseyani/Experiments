# Asset Allocation

## What It Is

Asset allocation is the practice of dividing your investment portfolio among different **asset classes** — most commonly stocks, bonds, and cash. Because each asset class tends to react differently to the same market conditions, spreading money across them reduces the chance that a single bad outcome wipes out your portfolio.

The right mix is highly personal: it depends on your [[time-horizon]] and your [[risk-tolerance]].

## How to Think About It

| If you have… | You might lean toward… |
|---|---|
| Long time horizon (20+ years) | More stocks — you have time to ride out downturns |
| Short time horizon (< 5 years) | More bonds/cash — capital preservation matters more |
| High risk tolerance | Heavier equity allocation |
| Low risk tolerance | Conservative (bond/cash-heavy) allocation |

There is no universally "correct" allocation. A common starting heuristic for long-term investors is 60% stocks / 40% bonds, but this should be adjusted based on your personal situation.

## Key Inputs

- **Time Horizon** — how long until you need the money. See [[time-horizon]].
- **Risk Tolerance** — how much volatility you can handle emotionally and financially. See [[risk-tolerance]].

## Why It Drifts (and Why That Matters)

Markets don't move in lockstep. Over time, one asset class will outperform others, skewing your portfolio away from your intended split. A portfolio that starts at 60/40 stocks/bonds can drift to 80/20 after a bull market — inadvertently making you more exposed to equity risk than you planned. That's why [[rebalancing]] is a core companion practice.

## Relationship to Diversification

Asset allocation answers *what proportion* goes where. [[Diversification]] goes further by also spreading within each asset class — holding many stocks in many sectors rather than concentrating in one.

## Tools

- Free risk-tolerance questionnaires are available on many investment websites; note that results can be biased toward the site's own products.
- **Target date funds** automatically adjust asset allocation over time, becoming more conservative as you approach a set year. Useful for hands-off investors.
