# Time Horizon

## What It Is

Your **investing time horizon** (also called *investing timeframe*) is the number of months, years, or decades you plan to invest before needing the money to achieve a financial goal. It is one of the two personal inputs — alongside [[risk-tolerance]] — that determines the right [[asset-allocation]] for you.

## Why It Matters

Time horizon fundamentally changes what kinds of risk you can take on:

| Horizon | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Long (10+ years)** | More time to recover from downturns → can hold more volatile assets (stocks) |
| **Medium (5–10 years)** | Balanced mix; begin shifting toward capital preservation as goal nears |
| **Short (< 5 years)** | Little time to recover losses → favor stable assets (bonds, cash) |

Short-term investments that turn into losses leave you with no time to recover before you need the funds. This is why retirees are advised to hold more bonds — their "runway" is shorter.

## Common Goal Timelines

- **Emergency fund** — 0 years (immediate access needed) → cash / money market only
- **Down payment on a house in 3 years** → conservative (bonds, high-yield savings)
- **College fund in 10 years** → moderate; shift toward bonds as the date approaches
- **Retirement in 30 years** → aggressive equity allocation early; gradually de-risk

## Time Horizon and Dollar Cost Averaging

A long time horizon is exactly what makes [[dollar-cost-averaging]] powerful. The more market cycles you invest through, the more your periodic purchases average out entry-point risk.

## Time Horizon and Rebalancing

As your horizon shrinks, your target [[asset-allocation]] should shift. Target date funds do this automatically. Individual investors must consciously re-evaluate their allocation as they approach their goal date and [[rebalancing|rebalance]] accordingly.
