# Compound Interest

## What It Is

**Compound growth** (also called compound interest) happens when you earn a return on money you invest *as well as* on the return your invested money earns. In other words: your gains generate their own gains.

The snowball metaphor captures it perfectly (investor.gov): *"like a snowball rolling on the ground and picking up additional snow with each rotation."* The longer it rolls, the larger each new layer of snow.

In fixed-income terms, the formula is: **A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)**

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Final amount |
| P | Principal (starting amount) |
| r | Annual interest rate (as a decimal) |
| n | Times interest compounds per year |
| t | Years |

For investments, "compounding" is less about a stated rate and more about reinvesting returns so that the base keeps growing.

## The Formula That Matters for Investors

**Regular investments + time → wealth** (investor.gov)

You don't need to start with a large amount — you need to start *early* and invest consistently. The earlier you start, the more compounding cycles you get.

## Why Time Is the Key Variable

The table below (from investor.gov, assumes 7% average annual return) shows how much you'd need to invest monthly to hit $500,000 or $1,000,000 by age 65:

| Age You Start | $500,000 by 65 | $1,000,000 by 65 |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | $127/month | $254/month |
| 25 | $209/month | $418/month |
| 35 | $441/month | $883/month |
| 45 | $1,016/month | $2,033/month |
| 55 | $3,016/month | $6,032/month |

Starting at 18 vs. 35 cuts your required monthly investment by more than 3× to reach the same goal — the difference is entirely due to compounding time.

## Compound Interest in Everyday Investing

- **Dividend reinvestment**: dividends from stocks re-purchase more shares, which pay more dividends
- **Interest on interest**: bond interest reinvested compounds over time
- **Index funds**: as the index grows, total market cap grows, and your gains compound within the fund
- **401(k)/IRA**: tax-advantaged accounts let compounding work uninterrupted by annual taxes on gains

## The Flip Side: Compound Interest on Debt

The same mechanism works *against* you on high-interest debt. A credit card at 20% APR compounding monthly grows debt rapidly. Paying off high-interest debt before investing is often the highest-return "investment" a beginner can make.
