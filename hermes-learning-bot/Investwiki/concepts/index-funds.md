# Index Funds and ETFs

## What They Are

**Index funds** and **ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)** are pooled investment vehicles that hold a basket of securities designed to track a market index (such as the S&P 500). They are the most commonly recommended investment vehicles for beginner investors.

| | Index Fund | ETF |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Pooled fund | Pooled fund |
| Trading | Once per day (at close) | Throughout the day like a stock |
| Minimum investment | Varies; many have $0 minimum | Cost of 1 share |
| Typical fees | Very low | Very low |
| Tax efficiency | Good | Slightly better than index funds |

Both give you exposure to dozens, hundreds, or thousands of companies in one purchase.

## Why They're the Beginner Recommendation

From the Investopedia "Sensibly, Suitably, Simply" framework:

1. **Start with the basics** — ETFs and index funds that track broad market indexes let you own a slice of the entire market rather than trying to pick individual winners.
2. **Keep costs low** — Index funds charge significantly lower fees (expense ratios) than actively managed funds. Every dollar paid in fees is a dollar not growing in your account.
3. **Diversification built-in** — A single S&P 500 index fund gives you exposure to 500 of the largest US companies across many sectors.
4. **Warren Buffett's advice** — The world's most famous investor has stated that most people would be better off investing in an S&P 500 index fund.

## The Investment Risk Ladder

Index funds can be built around different rungs of the risk ladder:

| Risk Level | Example Fund Type |
|---|---|
| Low | Bond index funds (Treasuries, corporate bonds) |
| Medium | Balanced funds (stocks + bonds) |
| Higher | US stock index funds (S&P 500, total market) |
| Even higher | Sector ETFs, small-cap funds, international funds |

Start at whatever risk level fits your [[time-horizon]] and [[risk-tolerance]], then move up the ladder as you learn.

## ETF History and Popularity

ETFs were launched in the early 1990s and became highly popular because they combined the diversification of mutual funds with the trading flexibility of stocks. They're now one of the most common entry points for new investors.

## Important Caveat: Not All Funds Are Diversified

A narrowly-focused ETF (e.g., a single-sector technology ETF) does *not* provide broad [[diversification]]. If you hold sector ETFs, check for overlapping holdings across your funds. Broad-market index funds (total stock market, S&P 500) provide the most automatic diversification.
