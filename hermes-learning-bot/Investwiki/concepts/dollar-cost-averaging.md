# Dollar Cost Averaging

## What It Is

Dollar cost averaging (DCA) is the strategy of **investing a fixed dollar amount on a regular schedule** — weekly, biweekly, monthly — regardless of what the market is doing. Rather than trying to find the "perfect" moment to invest, you invest consistently over time.

## How It Works

Because you invest a fixed dollar amount (not a fixed number of shares), you automatically:

- Buy **fewer shares** when prices are high
- Buy **more shares** when prices are low

Over many purchases, this tends to **lower your average cost per share** compared to making one large lump-sum investment at a potentially high price.

### Simple Example

| Month | Price per Share | Investment | Shares Bought |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | $50 | $100 | 2.00 |
| Feb | $25 | $100 | 4.00 |
| Mar | $40 | $100 | 2.50 |
| **Total** | — | **$300** | **8.50** |
| Avg cost/share | **$35.29** | vs. market avg | **$38.33** |

By buying more when prices were low (Feb), your average cost is below the simple average market price.

## Advantages

1. **Removes timing anxiety** — you don't need to predict market peaks or troughs
2. **Reduces volatility impact** — consistent investing smooths out the effect of market swings
3. **Behavioral benefit** — automatic investments reduce the temptation to market-time or panic-sell
4. **Accessible** — small, regular amounts are more achievable than one large lump sum

## Limitations

- Does not guarantee a profit or prevent loss in a declining market
- In a consistently rising market, a lump-sum investment at the start would outperform DCA (you'd buy all shares at low prices)
- Requires discipline and financial ability to keep investing through market downturns

## DCA and Time Horizon

DCA is most powerful over **long time horizons** — the more market cycles you invest through, the more the averaging effect works in your favor. See [[time-horizon]].

## DCA in Practice

Most people unknowingly practice DCA through 401(k) contributions: a fixed percentage of each paycheck goes into the market, regardless of conditions.
