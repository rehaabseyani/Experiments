# Venture Capital

Venture capital (VC) is a type of private investment fund that pools money from investors to fund early-stage, high-growth startup companies in exchange for equity (an ownership stake). Unlike buying shares on a stock exchange, venture capital investments are private — they are not publicly traded and are generally not available to everyday retail investors.

## How It Works

A VC fund is managed by **General Partners (GPs)** — the professional investors who make investment decisions. The investors who provide the money are called **Limited Partners (LPs)**. LPs commit capital to the fund and GPs deploy it into startups over a 3–5 year investment period. The overall fund life is typically 10 years, giving portfolio companies time to grow and eventually be acquired or go public (an "exit").

Returns to LPs come from those exit events. If a startup is acquired for a large sum, the fund distributes the proceeds back to LPs according to the fund's terms.

## Who Can Invest in VC Funds?

VC funds are **not open to ordinary retail investors**. To invest, you generally must be an **accredited investor** — which means meeting income or net worth thresholds set by the SEC:

- Income over $200,000 per year (or $300,000 jointly with a spouse) for the past two years, OR
- Net worth over $1 million, excluding your primary home

This restriction exists because private fund investments are illiquid, complex, and high-risk — regulators have determined they are not suitable for most retail investors.

## Risk Profile

VC is among the highest-risk investment categories:

- Most startups fail — a fund may lose money on 70–80% of its investments
- Returns are driven by a small number of "home run" exits (the power law)
- Investments are illiquid — you cannot sell your stake on a stock exchange
- Lock-up periods of 7–10 years are common

The potential for very high returns comes with very high risk of loss. Beginners building their portfolios should generally focus on [[diversification]] and [[index-funds]] before exploring alternative investments like VC.
