# Saving and Investing

## What Is Investing?

Both saving and investing mean setting aside money separate from what you spend on needs and wants.

- **Saving** → short-term goals or an emergency fund; typically held in a bank/credit union savings account; federally insured (FDIC); low but guaranteed return
- **Investing** → putting money into assets like stocks or bonds with the expectation of a return over time; involves risk; not FDIC-insured; higher potential return

Return from an investment may come from an *increase in the asset's value* or from *interest or dividend payments*. All investments involve risk — markets fluctuate. Some experts estimate a 7–10% annual return as a useful long-term benchmark for diversified US stock investments based on historic averages.

## Saving vs. Investing

| | Saving | Investing |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Short-term goals, emergency fund | Long-term wealth building |
| Risk | Very low (insured) | Higher (market risk) |
| Typical return | 0.5–5% | Historically 7–10% long-term |
| Liquidity | High | Variable |

**Emergency fund first:** before investing surplus income, build 3–6 months of expenses in cash — so you're never forced to sell investments at a bad time.

## The Roadmap to Saving and Investing

From investor.gov:

1. **Define your goals** — What are you investing for? Retirement, a home, education? Each goal has a different [[time-horizon]] and appropriate [[asset-allocation]].
2. **Figure out your finances** — Understand your income, expenses, and what you can realistically set aside each month.
3. **Small savings add up** — Consistent small contributions harness [[compound-interest]] over time. Starting early matters more than starting with a large amount.
4. **Pay off high-interest debt first** — High-interest debt (credit cards) often charges more than investments can earn. This is generally step zero before serious investing.

## How Compounding Turns Savings into Wealth

The power of compound growth is why *when* you start matters as much as *how much* you invest. From investor.gov (assumes 7% annual return):

| Age You Start | $500,000 by Age 65 | $1,000,000 by Age 65 |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | $127/month | $254/month |
| 25 | $209/month | $418/month |
| 35 | $441/month | $883/month |
| 45 | $1,016/month | $2,033/month |
| 55 | $3,016/month | $6,032/month |

**The formula:** regular investments + time → wealth

See [[compound-interest]] for the full mechanics.

## Getting Started

The most important action is to **start**. [[Dollar-cost-averaging]] — investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule — is one of the simplest ways to begin without needing to time the market. [[Index-funds]] make this easy: low cost, instant diversification, no stock-picking required.
