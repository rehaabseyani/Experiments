# Asset Classes

## What They Are

An **asset class** is a group of investments that share similar characteristics, behave similarly in the market, and are subject to similar regulations. The three major asset classes are:

| Asset Class | What It Is | Risk Level | Typical Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stocks (Equities)** | Ownership shares in a company | High | Growth |
| **Bonds (Fixed Income)** | Loans to governments or companies that pay interest | Medium | Stability / income |
| **Cash & Cash Equivalents** | Savings accounts, money market funds, T-bills | Low | Liquidity / capital preservation |

Other asset classes include real estate, commodities, and alternative investments — these are less common for beginners.

## How Asset Classes Relate

Different asset classes tend to **react differently to the same economic conditions**:

- When stock markets fall (risk-off environment), investors often move to bonds, pushing bond prices up
- When interest rates rise, bond prices fall — but stocks may or may not follow
- Cash maintains value but loses ground to inflation over time

This imperfect correlation is the foundation of [[diversification]] — holding multiple asset classes reduces the overall volatility of a portfolio.

## Investment Vehicles Within Each Class

You don't have to buy individual stocks or bonds. Funds let you own a slice of many:

- **Mutual Funds** — pooled investment vehicles; actively or passively managed
- **ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)** — like mutual funds but trade on exchanges like stocks
- **Index Funds** — passively track a market index (e.g., S&P 500)
- **Target Date Funds** — automatically shift from stocks to bonds as a target year approaches; handles [[rebalancing]] automatically

**Important caveat:** A single narrowly-focused fund (e.g., a technology-sector ETF) does not provide broad diversification on its own. Check top holdings across your funds for overlap.

## Asset Classes and Allocation

Your [[asset-allocation]] is, at its core, a decision about what percentage of your portfolio sits in each asset class. That decision is driven by your [[time-horizon]] and [[risk-tolerance]].
